ACADEMIC PROJECT / RUHR UNIVERSITY BOCHUM

Reading a landscape through its rivers.

A computational geomorphology study of the Sorbas region in southeastern Spain, using the Copernicus GLO-30 elevation model and TopoToolbox in MATLAB.

From terrain to evidence

The DEM was reprojected to UTM zone 30N at 30 m resolution. Channels were extracted above a 1 km² contributing-area threshold, then filtered to select the largest remaining connected network.

  • Calculated normalized channel steepness and chi using an adopted reference concavity of approximately 0.354.
  • Compared sinuosity over 5 km and 2.5 km reaches.
  • Identified 17 knickpoint candidates using a 30 m vertical tolerance.
  • Compared drainage metrics with geological mapping and swath profiles.

What the results showed

The longest trunk extends approximately 66 km. Steepness, sinuosity, and knickpoint patterns vary across the network, supporting an interpretation that considers multiple geological and landscape controls. A knickpoint alone does not establish active tectonics.
